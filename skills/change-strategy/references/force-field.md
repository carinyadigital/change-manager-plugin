# Forces for and against — running a force-field session

Force-field analysis is a classic idea from the social psychologist Kurt Lewin:
any situation is held in place by forces pushing for change and forces
holding it back. Change happens when the driving forces are strengthened or —
often more effectively — the restraining forces are reduced. The concept is
free to describe; this guide and its template are the plugin's own.

## When it helps

- Leaders disagree about how hard the change will be.
- The strategy needs to show why it focuses where it does.
- Resistance is expected but not yet understood.

## Running it (45–60 minutes with the sponsor and leaders)

1. **State the change** in one sentence from `scope.md`, as the "from → to".
2. **List driving forces** — what is pushing for the change (customer demand,
   cost, regulation, frustration with today's process, leader commitment).
3. **List restraining forces** — what is holding today's way in place
   (habits, targets that reward the old way, workload, change history, loss of
   status or skill, system gaps, competing changes).
4. **Rate each force** 1 (weak) to 3 (strong), from evidence where possible.
5. **Choose actions.** For the two or three strongest restraining forces, what
   would reduce them? For the strongest driving forces, how will the change
   use them?
6. **Carry it forward.** Restraining forces become risks
   (`change-risk-assessment`) or approach choices in the strategy; actions get
   an owner.

## Watch-outs

- Pushing harder on driving forces tends to increase resistance; reducing
  restraining forces usually works better.
- Ratings are judgements. Record who was in the room and the evidence behind
  any 3.
- Name forces as conditions, not people ("targets reward call speed", not
  "the Brisbane team leader").
