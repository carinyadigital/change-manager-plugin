# Choosing a method — framework selector

Original guidance written for this plugin. It paraphrases when each well-known
approach tends to fit and links to [methods.md](methods.md)
for attribution, owners and reuse limits. It does not reproduce any owner's
steps, tools or wording.

## Start here

1. **If the organisation already uses a method** (practice profile `method`),
   use it. Consistency with how leaders already talk about change beats a
   theoretically better fit. Note any gaps the chosen method leaves and cover
   them in plain terms.
2. **If not, default to method-neutral.** Plan around the plain-English
   elements every approach shares: a clear reason, visible leaders, knowing
   what changes for me, being able to do it, support on the day, and
   reinforcement afterwards. Borrow one lens from the list below where it
   genuinely helps, and name it with attribution.
3. **Never mix vocabularies on one page.** Pick one set of terms for
   employee- and leader-facing material.

## When each lens tends to help

| Situation | Lens that tends to help | Why (in our words) | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Many individuals must each change how they work (new system, new process) and you need to see where people are stuck | Individual change barrier view (ADKAR-informed) | Breaks one person's change into a sequence, so you can act on the first weak point rather than doing everything for everyone | Proprietary; describe and link only. Label outputs "individual change barrier diagnostic (ADKAR-informed)" unless licensed |
| The change needs leaders to build momentum across a large organisation, and urgency is low | Leader-led campaign (Kotter-style) | Puts weight on urgency, a guiding group, volunteers and visible early wins | Can become a top-down announcement plan; still plan for individual capability |
| The change involves loss — roles, teams, identity, a familiar way of working, a closing site | Transition view (Bridges) | Separates the external event from the internal adjustment and makes "what's ending" explicit | Proprietary materials; describe and link. Not a planning method on its own |
| The change is about behaviour and culture more than tools | Four conditions for behaviour (McKinsey Influence Model) | Checks conviction, formal reinforcement, skills and role models together | Cite; don't reproduce diagnostics |
| Public sector or a regulated environment wanting a documented, stage-based approach | APS Change Framework, ACMP Standard as a lifecycle map | Gives a recognised structure and artefact set that governance bodies accept | Check licence terms for business use of the ACMP Standard |
| The worry is whether the change will last after the project ends | Sustainability factors (NHS Sustainability Model) | A short self-check of the conditions that keep a change in place | Verify licence per document; use the plugin's own sustainability check for scoring |
| Strong forces for and against the change need weighing | Force-field analysis (Lewin) | Simple way to list driving and restraining forces with leaders | Classic concept; free to describe |

## Sizing the change

Use the impact register and stakeholder register to size the change before
choosing how much effort each approach area needs:

| Signal | Smaller change | Larger change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Highest group headline score | 1–2 | 3–4 |
| Groups at high priority | One or two | Several, or one very large group |
| People affected | Tens | Hundreds or more, several locations |
| Roles redesigned, removed or created | None | Any |
| Sponsor commitment and health check | Committed, Healthy | Gaps, Some or Material concern |
| Other changes hitting the same groups | Few | Saturated cells on the heatmap |

A larger change usually needs a champion network, structured readiness
checks, manager toolkits and a longer reinforcement period. A smaller one may
need only briefings, a job aid and a support route. Record the reasoning, not
just the label.
