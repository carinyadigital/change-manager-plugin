# Common people-side risks — prompt list

Original prompt list written for this plugin. For each of the nine risk
areas, the risks that come up again and again, the evidence that suggests
one is real, and mitigations that usually help. Use it to **look** for risks;
log only those the evidence for this initiative supports, written in its own
specifics. A risk copied from this list without evidence is noise.

| Area | Common risk (cause → event → consequence) | Evidence that it's real | Mitigations that usually help |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Competing changes and saturation | Because several changes land on the same groups in the same months, people may not absorb this one, so adoption stalls or errors rise | Saturated or watch cells on the heatmap; leaders saying "not another one" | Re-sequence with the portfolio owner; combine touchpoints; cut non-essential asks |
| Capacity to take part | Because rosters have no slack and backfill isn't funded, people may miss training and engagement, so they arrive at go-live unprepared | No backfill in the resource plan; low attendance at early sessions; peak season overlap | Fund backfill; shorter, repeated sessions across shifts; managers release time in writing |
| Sponsorship weakness | Because the sponsor is not visible to affected groups, people may doubt the change is a priority, so commitment stays low | Change health check S2 or S3 low; sponsor actions missed on the roadmap; sponsor change | Sponsor roadmap with a few visible moments; coalition of line leaders; escalate the gap |
| Change fatigue | Because recent changes were long and disruptive, people may disengage, so feedback dries up and quiet non-use rises | Falling pulse response rates; "we've heard it all before"; poor change history | Fewer, clearer messages; early visible proof; acknowledge what the last change cost |
| Complexity | Because the new way changes several things at once for one group, people may not reach proficiency by go-live, so service drops | Several dimensions at 3+ for one group; long proficiency time in the TNA | Phase the change; more practice; floor support; adjusted targets during the learning curve |
| History of failed change | Because the last similar change was rolled back, people may wait it out, so the old way persists | Organisational readiness OR-C1 to OR-C3 low; parallel old systems still running | Remove the old way on a date; leaders say what is different this time; early wins shared |
| Resistance from specific groups | Because a group loses status, skill or autonomy, it may resist actively or quietly, so adoption in that group lags | Loss lens findings; pulse "I can see how this will help" low in one group; workarounds | Acknowledge the loss; involve the group in local design; transition plan; manager one-to-ones |
| Timing | Because go-live falls in a peak period, leave season or month-end, people may not have time to learn or get support, so errors spike | Go-live date against the business calendar; leave patterns | Move the date or the wave; extra support for the peak; freeze other asks |
| Manager capability | Because managers learn the change at the same time as their teams, they may not answer questions or coach, so teams lose confidence | Managers not in the TNA; leader-briefed criterion Amber; pulse P-C4 low | Brief and train managers first; manager packs; coaching guides; a manager Q&A line |

## Also check

| Risk | Evidence | Usually owned by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consultation obligations not met in time | Role, location or hours changes; enterprise agreement clauses | HR / employee relations |
| Key people leave during the change | Critical roles with no backup; retention concerns | Business owner, HR |
| Practice environment or job aids not ready in time | Dependencies in the TNA and training plan | Training lead, project manager |
| Support model too thin for night shifts or remote sites | Rota gaps in the go-live plan | Change lead, service owner |
| Data or access problems mistaken for resistance | Hypercare themes tagged access or data | Project manager, IT |

## Writing a good risk

- **Specific to this initiative:** names the group, the timing and the
  mechanism.
- **Has a trigger:** a measurable sign that says act now (from the measurement
  plan or readiness checklist where possible).
- **Has one owner:** a role who will act, not "the project".
- **Not a delivery risk in disguise:** if the consequence is about build,
  test or budget, it belongs in the project risk register — record only the
  people consequence here.
