# Running a change retrospective

Original guidance written for this plugin. Use it at a phase boundary or at
closeout to run a short session that turns experience into lessons a future
team can act on. Outputs go into the lessons-learned log as entries.

## Ground rules (say them at the start)

- We are looking at approaches and conditions, not judging people.
- What is said stays with the change team unless we agree otherwise.
- Every "avoid" gets a "do instead". Every "repeat" says why it worked.
- One lesson per sticky note or line.

## Who to invite

Change lead, project manager, two or three people leaders from impacted
groups, two or three champions, the comms and training leads, and — for the
closeout session — the sponsor for the last 15 minutes. Six to ten people
works best. For frontline voices that can't attend, gather two or three
prompts through champions beforehand.

## Session outline (60–90 minutes)

| Time | Step | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 min | Purpose and ground rules | Read the rules aloud |
| 10 min | Timeline | Lay out the key moments of the phase together (announcement, training, go-live, first payday…) |
| 20 min | What helped, what got in the way | Silent writing, then group by theme; use the prompts below |
| 15 min | Pick the lessons that matter | Each person picks three; discuss the top five to eight |
| 15 min | Make them actionable | For each: what to do next time, who will carry it, evidence |
| 5 min | Close | What happens to these lessons and when people will see them |

Run it within two weeks of the phase ending — detail fades fast.

## Prompt questions by phase

### Prepare

- How well did we understand who was affected and how, before we planned?
- Did the sponsor and leaders agree on why, and did people hear it from them?
- What did we learn too late that we could have learned earlier?
- Were the right people involved in designing how the change would work?

### Manage (build-up to go-live)

- Which messages landed, and how do we know? Which didn't?
- Did managers have what they needed, early enough, to lead their teams?
- Was training at the right time, depth and format for each group?
- How well did feedback come back, and did we close the loop?
- Where did resistance come from, and did we read it correctly?
- How did this change interact with other changes hitting the same teams?

### Go-live and hypercare

- What did people struggle with in the first week — and was it people,
  process, system, data or access?
- Was the support model in the right places, at the right times, for every
  shift?
- How quickly did fixes reach staff?
- Was the go/no-go decision made on good evidence?

### Sustain

- What stuck, and what slipped back? Why?
- Was the old way removed at the right time?
- Who owns the measures now, and is anyone looking at them?
- What would we tell the next team on day one?

## Turning notes into entries

*Illustrative examples of the shape only — don't copy their content; each
real lesson comes from this initiative's notes and evidence.*

| Raw note | Usable lesson |
| --- | --- |
| "Comms could have been better" | "Brief team leaders two days before all-staff messages so they can answer questions on the day." |
| "Training was too early" | "Schedule hands-on training in the two to three weeks before go-live; earlier sessions needed a refresher." |
| "The sponsor was great" | "Sponsor visiting each site in announcement week lifted understanding; repeat for any change over 100 people." |

Each entry still needs evidence (what showed this) and an owner (who will
make sure the next initiative uses it).
