# Individual change barrier diagnostic (ADKAR-informed)

Original diagnostic questions and intervention map written for this plugin.
The five elements follow the sequence described by Prosci's ADKAR® model —
awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, reinforcement. **ADKAR® is a registered
trademark of Prosci, Inc.** This file does not reproduce Prosci's assessment
questions, scoring sheets or materials, and outputs using it must not be
labelled "ADKAR assessment" unless the organisation holds a Prosci licence (see
[methods.md](../../../references/methods.md)). The loss lens draws on the idea
of transition described by William Bridges Associates, in our own words.

## How to use it

1. Work with a **group**, not a person. Use evidence: what people say in
   sessions, champion feedback, survey comments, questions to the help desk,
   behaviour (workarounds, non-attendance, low usage).
2. Rate each element 1 (absent) to 5 (strong) from that evidence, in order.
   Write the evidence next to every score. No evidence = do not score; mark it
   as a gap to find out.
3. The **barrier point** is the first element, in sequence, rated 3 or below.
   Act there first. Training people who do not yet see why the change matters
   rarely works; nor does asking for commitment from people who have not been
   told what is changing.
4. Run the loss lens alongside — it often explains a low desire score.
5. Re-rate after the intervention has had time to work.

## Diagnostic questions

Ask these of the evidence (and, where useful, of managers and champions). They
are prompts for judgement, not a survey.

### 1. Doesn't understand why (awareness)

- Can people in this group explain, in their own words, why the change is
  happening and why now?
- Do they know what happens if nothing changes?
- Have they heard the reasons from someone they regard as credible — and more
  than once?
- Are rumours or a different story filling the gap?

### 2. Doesn't want to (desire)

- When people talk about the change, do they talk about what's in it for them
  or only about what they lose?
- Is their direct manager visibly in favour, neutral or against it?
- Are there real costs to them — workload, status, income, autonomy, job
  security — that nobody has acknowledged?
- Has previous change here gone badly, and does that history come up?
- Do they feel they had any say in how it affects them?

### 3. Doesn't know how (knowledge)

- Do people know what they will do differently on day one — tasks, systems,
  decisions, handoffs?
- Has training been offered at the right time, at the right depth, for this
  group's actual work?
- Do they know where to find instructions or who to ask?

### 4. Can't yet (ability)

- When people try the new way, can they do it at the expected standard and
  speed?
- Have they had enough practice in realistic conditions?
- Are there practical blockers — access, equipment, time, rosters, conflicting
  targets, broken processes?
- Is there someone nearby to help when they get stuck?

### 5. Not reinforced (reinforcement)

- Are people recognised when they use the new way, or does nobody notice?
- Do targets, reports, approvals and performance measures still reward the
  old way?
- Is the old system, form or workaround still available and easier?
- Do managers check and coach, or has attention moved on?

### Loss lens — what's ending

- What are people being asked to let go of — a skill they were proud of, a
  relationship, a routine, a status, a sense of control?
- Has anyone named that loss openly and acknowledged it matters?
- Are people stuck in the in-between — the old way gone, the new way not yet
  working — with no clear end date?
- Is there anything that marks the ending and the new start?

## Intervention map

| Barrier | Typical signals | Interventions that fit | What does not work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Doesn't understand why | "Nobody told us why", rumours, "it's just cost cutting" | Sponsor or senior leader explains the reasons in person; manager-led team discussion; plain-English case for change; answer the hard questions openly | More training; detailed process documents |
| Doesn't want to | "What's in it for me?", "we tried this before", quiet non-participation | Acknowledge real costs; involve the group in shaping how it lands locally; manager one-to-ones; peer stories from groups already through it; address the specific loss; escalate genuine design issues | Repeating the business case louder; pressure without listening |
| Doesn't know how | "I don't know what I'm meant to do", lots of basic questions | Targeted training for this group's tasks; job aids; demos; clear "what changes for you" briefings | Motivational messages |
| Can't yet | Errors, slow work, workarounds, "the system won't let me" | Practice time; coaching and floor support; fix access and process blockers; adjust targets during the learning curve | More classroom training on what they already know |
| Not reinforced | Slipping back, old forms reappearing, "nobody checks" | Recognition; align measures and approvals; retire the old system or workaround; manager follow-up; share early results | One-off celebrations with no follow-through |
| Loss (any element) | Grief, anger, nostalgia, disengagement | Name and acknowledge what is ending; mark the ending; give a clear timeline for the in-between; extra support and patience during it | Telling people to "get on board" |

## Not resistance

Some signals look like resistance but are something else. Route them, do not
log them as barriers:

- **A defect or design flaw** people are rightly pointing out → raise with the
  project; log as feedback, not resistance.
- **Individual grievance, performance or conduct matter** → line manager and
  HR process. The change record does not hold it.
- **Wellbeing concern** → line manager and the organisation's support
  services.
- **Industrial relations or consultation obligations** → HR and employee
  relations; may change timing or approach.
